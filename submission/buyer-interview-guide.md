# Buyer interview and pilot guide

This is a future research instrument. No interviews, outreach, purchase commitments or pilot results have been completed or claimed. Do not send messages or schedule interviews without Shivam's authorisation.

Speak with three people who actually coordinate estate services or community food support, including at least one person responsible for a budget. Allow 25–30 minutes. Ask permission before taking notes or recording, explain that participation creates no sales obligation, and request no names or identifying details about people receiving assistance.

Start with: “I’m exploring how organisations prepare for scheduled hawker-centre closures. I’d like to understand your current process before showing you a prototype. There are no right answers; it would be useful to learn if this is not a significant problem for you.”

For the first fifteen minutes, leave the product closed:

1. “Tell me about the most recent closure you had to respond to. What triggered work for your team?” Ask for the actual sequence, people involved and documents used.
2. “What did you need to decide, and which decisions belonged to someone else?” Distinguish notifications, cleaning scheduling, welfare outreach and meal provision.
3. “Which information was hardest to establish? How did you resolve it?” Look for verified alternatives, routes, venue capacity, demand and dietary needs without suggesting these answers first.
4. “Roughly how much staff time did that instance take, and what makes that estimate credible?” Ask for calendar entries or task records only if they can be shared without personal data.
5. “How often did this work arise in the last year? What happened when your team did nothing?” An infrequent or low-consequence task may not justify a subscription.
6. “Which existing tools or services are good enough? What would be inconvenient about changing the process?” Treat spreadsheets and doing nothing as real alternatives.

Then show one dated scenario for five minutes. Say: “This uses old census counts and approximate distances. Proposed venues, costs and participation are assumptions.” Ask the person to explain the output in their own words. Record any confusion between exposure and need, or proposed meals and actual meals.

Ask: “What would you verify before using this? Which result would you reject? What is missing from the record you need to pass to a colleague?” Do not ask “Wouldn't this save you time?”

In the final five minutes, explore the buying process: “Who could authorise a small paid evaluation? What budget would it come from? What evidence and procurement steps would be required? What service expectations would make an engagement too risky?” Only after understanding this process, present the S$1,000 scoped-pilot and S$250–500 monthly price hypotheses. Record objections and concrete next steps. Polite interest is not a commitment or revenue.

Keep an anonymised research log with role, date, observed workflow, frequency, evidence for time estimates, existing tools, most important objection, buyer authority, agreed next step and permission to follow up. Quote only with explicit permission. Separate observations from interpretations.

For a willing pilot operator, agree in writing on one closure, scope, source date, task checklist, operator responsibilities and success criteria before starting. Compare equal tasks using the existing process and HawkerBridge; account for learning effects and the small sample. Record preparation minutes, corrections, critical omissions, verified/rejected venues, support minutes and repeat-use willingness. Proposed targets in [what's next](whats-next.md) are decision gates, not promises. No participant should lose access to assistance for the sake of the evaluation.
